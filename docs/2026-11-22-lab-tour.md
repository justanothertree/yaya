# The Lab tour: friends on a call, on the Lab's board together

Evan, 22 Nov 2026: _"my favorite things are sharing experiences, interacting, creating and sharing
with friends. Prioritize multiplayer … a shared lobby where friends see each other as shadow
players, so touring the Lab on a call with friends feels like Mario Party."_ This is item 1 of
that list. It is built on what the call already carries, in four releasable steps, each its own
commit:

1. **The board tells the truth** — this file's first section. ✅ step 1
2. **See each other** — a tour strip above the kinds: a shadow token per person on the call, where
   each is, and Go.
3. **The tour** — Follow: a standing, local choice to be taken wherever a friend goes, which
   pauses rather than pulls you out of a game.
4. **Dice** — 🎲 Surprise us.

## Why none of it adds a message to the wire

Everything the tour shows is already on the call's channel. `where` says which page each person
is on and, behind the Lab's friends-only gate (`src/lab/social/gate.ts`), which toy; the call's
roster says who is in the call; `speaking` is measured from the audio you are already receiving.
So the tour is new READINGS of old messages: no new party kind, no new body field, and old and new
clients interoperate unchanged. The one thing that will add traffic is Follow (step 3), and only as
the ordinary `where` that any pick of a toy already sends.

## Step 1 — the board tells the truth

### One rule on the receiving side

**Nothing finer than the page is drawn unless YOUR OWN Lab gate allows it.** The gate has always
been checked by the SENDER, before anything goes out: a toy, and a spot on it, go to a call only if
everyone else present is the sender's friend. Friendship is not transitive, so in one call Bo can
be friends with everybody while Ann is not friends with Cy — Bo's gate opens and his toy goes to
the whole channel, Ann's stays shut. Every Lab surface already asked the viewer's gate before
drawing (the picker's dots, the roster's link, the call's game rooms) except one: PartyCursors drew
a friend's toy-relative pointer and their tap rings whatever the viewer's gate said.

`src/party/cursorPlace.ts` is that decision, out of the rAF loop and pure: `cursorPlace` answers
stage / page / hidden for a pointer sample, `tapShown` answers whether a ring is drawn. A refused
toy-relative sample falls back to the page position it also carries — exactly what any other page
of the site shows.

Measured on the dev call (`#dev-call`, two tabs of different widths so a stage position and a
page position land in different places), Ann's tab with her gate shut (`kind=lounge`) and Bo's
open (`kind=dm`), Bo moving over Four in a row's stage:

|                             | main                         | this change                 |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Bo sent a toy-relative spot | yes                          | yes                         |
| Bo's cursor on Ann's tab    | **0 px from the stage spot** | 0 px from the page position |
| Bo's tap ring on Ann's tab  | **visible**                  | hidden                      |

### The roster is the list

`onToysNow` (gate.ts) draws somebody on a toy only while they are still on the call's roster, and a
`where` from an id that never joined the call is never drawn at all. The roster drops a person on
their `bye` — the Leave button, or a tab closed — while leaving tells the party nothing (`leave()`
sends the call a `bye` and the party no `gone`), so the party kept a friend who had hung up on their
toy until its 16 s sweep. Measured with Ann, Bo and Cy on a call and Bo pressing Leave: Bo's dot left
Ann's picker after **14,455 ms** on main and at the first look, **9 ms**, here; Cy's stayed.

⚠️ **A CRASH IS NOT FASTER, and the first version of this section said it was.** A crashed tab
sends no `bye`, and WebRTC never reports it closed — voiceSession marks it reconnecting and keeps it
on the roster for the rest of the call — so it leaves a toy at the party's 16 s sweep exactly as
before: **14,211 ms on main, 14,457 ms here**. The first measurement said 4,064 ms because the dev
call expired a silent tab from its roster after 4 s, which no real call does; the review that caught
it read voiceSession's `dropPeer` callers. The dev call now changes its roster only the way a real
one does — on a `bye`, never on silence (`src/dev/labCall.ts`, and CLAUDE.md §2) — and both
figures above were taken with that faithful double on main as well as here.

### A friend on another toy, over a board you do not have

39 toys are built from the page rather than on a stage, so a pointer over one of them carries only
a page position — which on your screen lands on a board they are not looking at. On the Lab, a
friend known (by their `where`) to be on a different toy is now hidden below your `.lab-line` and
shown above it, over the kinds and the picker you share. Measured with Bo on Chess (no stage) and
Ann on Four in a row: Bo over his picker — visible on Ann's tab before and after; Bo over his board
— **visible on main, hidden here**.

It is an approximation and says so in the code: the line is measured on your page and the pointer
on theirs, and whatever above it differs between you (Your games is your own list) moves the two
apart. That is the error every page-position cursor on the site already carries.

### What anybody can now see

- **The toy on screen and the kind chosen are pressed.** Both rows set `aria-pressed` and nothing
  drew it — the Lab's only pressed style was `.lab-controls`' — so the one fact a picker exists to
  show was told to a screen reader and nobody else. Same tint as the toy's own controls.
- **In a call, you are on the board**: a hollow ring in your own colour (the colour your cursor is
  on everybody else's screen) on the toy your call is being told, while your gate allows and you
  are sharing.
- **Friends land.** A friend's initial pops onto the toy they reach — only when they have
  actually arrived (GpuLab keeps where everyone was at the last look). A dot also mounts when
  somebody else leaving lets a friend who was there all along into the first three, and that
  friend did not move: measured with five on one toy, the friend who moved landed on Chess and the
  one newly shown on Four in a row did not pop. Flattened by the site's reduce-motion switch
  (measured: 0.001s) and by `prefers-reduced-motion` (read from the CSS — it cannot be emulated in
  the pane).
- **"👥 with Ann and Bo"** leads the line above the toy, for a phone player scrolled down at the
  stage (`withWords` in `src/lab/social/tour.ts`). Three long names wrap inside the card rather
  than keeping the label pill's nowrap: measured at 375 with three 19-character names, two lines,
  right edge 344 inside a card ending at 365.

### Viewports

375 (touch emulated), 820 (with every `(pointer: coarse)` rule applied, CLAUDE.md §6) and 1440,
in a call with a friend on the same toy: `mobileAudit` 0 sideways, 0 off-edge, 0 clipped, 0
undersized at all three, after being made to speak first (an injected 16×16 control and a 600px
child in a 120px box named at every width). 0 spills at 375 and 1440. At 820 one spill, the call
dock's Leave button 10 px past the dock — **identical on main**, so it is not this change's; named
here rather than fixed in it.

## Follow-ups named, not built

- A structural receive gate: a `labPart` filter inside party.ts's receive path, so a new Lab
  surface cannot forget the rule above. One rule in one module is enough while there is one
  surface that draws pointers.
- A stage anchor for the 39 page-built toys, so their pointers can be toy-relative too.
- The bus tour (after step 4): a guide, a 🎲 roll or a vote between three toys, a "take me there"
  ready check, a star of each stop. It needs a new party kind with room.ts's host/seq/hello
  pattern, budgeted against the call's whole Realtime quota.

## Open questions for Evan

1. **A 🫱 button inside the Lab?** Nothing toy-level shows until everybody presses the dock's 🫱,
   which is off by default and resets every call. A second button bound to the SAME switch,
   labelled in words, is probably the biggest single lever on the tour feeling like a party — and
   it overturns CallDock's documented decision that the switch lives with the call. Not built.
2. **Should a leader see who is following them?** The house rule (`shared.ts`, `together.ts`) is
   never; Follow keeps it.
3. **Is Realtime's project-wide "Allow public access" switched off?** A dashboard setting with no
   recorded answer (docs/2026-08-10-realtime-auth-remaining-channels.sql). The tour sends nothing
   new, but it encourages longer sharing.
