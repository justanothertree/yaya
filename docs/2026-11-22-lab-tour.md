# The Lab tour: friends on a call, on the Lab's board together

Evan, 22 Nov 2026: _"my favorite things are sharing experiences, interacting, creating and sharing
with friends. Prioritize multiplayer … a shared lobby where friends see each other as shadow
players, so touring the Lab on a call with friends feels like Mario Party."_ This is item 1 of
that list. It is built on what the call already carries, in four releasable steps, each its own
commit:

1. **The board tells the truth** — this file's first section. ✅ step 1
2. **See each other** — a tour strip above the kinds: a shadow token per person on the call, where
   each is, and Go. ✅ step 2
3. **The tour** — Follow: a standing, local choice to be taken wherever a friend goes, which
   pauses rather than pulls you out of a game. ✅ step 3
4. **Dice** — 🎲 Surprise us. ✅ step 4
5. **The bus** — a party tour: the guide rolls three toys, friends say Take me there, the bus goes,
   and the turn passes on. ✅ step 5 (rules in `bus.ts`, words in `busWords.ts`, the page in
   `useBus.ts` and `BusPanel.tsx`)

## Steps 1–4 add nothing to the wire; the bus adds one kind, `bus`

Everything steps 1–4 show is already on the call's channel. `where` says which page each person
is on and, behind the Lab's friends-only gate (`src/lab/social/gate.ts`), which toy; the call's
roster says who is in the call; `speaking` is measured from the audio you are already receiving.
So steps 1–4 are new READINGS of old messages: no new party kind, no new body field, and old and
new clients interoperate unchanged. Follow (step 3) adds traffic only as the ordinary `where` that
any pick of a toy already sends.

The bus (step 5) is the first thing on the Lab that needs a conversation, so it adds ONE party
kind, `bus`, and nothing to any existing body: a guide's roll, a pick, a Take me there and the
host's state of the tour. Old clients ignore the kind (party listeners handle the kinds they know),
never ride, never host and are never waited for. With no tour it sends nothing at all; its rates
and caps are in `bus.ts` and the section below.

## Step 1 — the board tells the truth

### One rule on the receiving side

**Nothing finer than the page is drawn unless YOUR OWN Lab gate allows it.** The gate has always
been checked by the SENDER, before anything goes out: a toy, and a spot on it, go to a call only if
everyone else present is the sender's friend. Friendship is not transitive, so in one call Bo can
be friends with everybody while Ann is not friends with Cy — Bo's gate opens and his toy goes to
the whole channel, Ann's stays shut. Every Lab surface already asked the viewer's gate before
drawing (the picker's dots, the roster's link, the call's game rooms) except one: PartyCursors drew
a friend's toy-relative pointer and their tap rings whatever the viewer's gate said.

`src/party/cursorPlace.ts` is that decision, out of the rAF loop and pure: `cursorPlace` answers
stage / page / hidden for a pointer sample, `tapShown` answers whether a ring is drawn. A refused
toy-relative sample falls back to the page position it also carries — exactly what any other page
of the site shows.

Measured on the dev call (`#dev-call`, two tabs of different widths so a stage position and a
page position land in different places), Ann's tab with her gate shut (`kind=lounge`) and Bo's
open (`kind=dm`), Bo moving over Four in a row's stage:

| Bo's cursor on Ann's tab    | **0 px from the stage spot** | 0 px from the page position |

### The roster is the list

`onToysNow` (gate.ts) draws somebody on a toy only while they are still on the call's roster, and a
`where` from an id that never joined the call is never drawn at all. The roster drops a person on
their `bye` — the Leave button, or a tab closed — while leaving tells the party nothing (`leave()`
sends the call a `bye` and the party no `gone`), so the party kept a friend who had hung up on their
toy until its 16 s sweep. Measured with Ann, Bo and Cy on a call and Bo pressing Leave: Bo's dot left
Ann's picker after **14,455 ms** on main and at the first look, **9 ms**, here; Cy's stayed.

⚠️ **A CRASH IS NOT FASTER, and the first version of this section said it was.** A crashed tab
sends no `bye`, and WebRTC never reports it closed — voiceSession marks it reconnecting and keeps it
on the roster for the rest of the call — so it leaves a toy at the party's 16 s sweep exactly as
before: **14,211 ms on main, 14,457 ms here**. The first measurement said 4,064 ms because the dev
call expired a silent tab from its roster after 4 s, which no real call does; the review that caught
it read voiceSession's `dropPeer` callers. The dev call now changes its roster only the way a real
one does — on a `bye`, never on silence (`src/dev/labCall.ts`, and CLAUDE.md §2) — and both
figures above were taken with that faithful double on main as well as here.

### A friend on another toy, over a board you do not have

39 toys are built from the page rather than on a stage, so a pointer over one of them carries only
a page position — which on your screen lands on a board they are not looking at. On the Lab, a
friend known (by their `where`) to be on a different toy is now hidden below your `.lab-line` and
shown above it, over the kinds and the picker you share. Measured with Bo on Chess (no stage) and
Ann on Four in a row: Bo over his picker — visible on Ann's tab before and after; Bo over his board
— **visible on main, hidden here**.

It is an approximation and says so in the code: the line is measured on your page and the pointer
on theirs, and whatever above it differs between you (Your games is your own list) moves the two
apart. That is the error every page-position cursor on the site already carries.

## Step 2 — see each other

`src/lab/social/TourStrip.tsx`, between Your games and the kinds, decided by `tourOf` in
`tour.ts`. Three things it can be:

- **Nothing**: not in a call, or not on the Lab page (the Lab in a canvas window over another page
  is not touring).
- **One sentence, when it may not show places** — the gate's reason, said for the first time
  anywhere (`Verdict.why` was computed and never shown). Lounge, a stranger present (naming
  nobody), a room whose kind was never said, friends still loading, nobody else there yet — and,
  gate open but you not sharing, the call's names and what pressing 🫱 shares, and with whom:
  "everyone on the call, including anyone who joins later: your pointer, the page you're on and
  anything you open, and which toy, while everyone here is your friend". The switch is the
  call's, not the Lab's, so the sentence names the call as its audience rather than the friends
  here now — a review caught the first draft saying "share with them", a smaller audience than
  the switch has.
- **The strip**: a token per person in the roster's order, you first. Each token is the colour that
  person already is everywhere (`hueFor`: their cursor, their nav ring, their picker dot), their
  initial and their cursor's shape. Filled when they are with you, tinted when they are elsewhere,
  dashed when they are saying nothing. Talking is a still ring and the word "talking", never colour
  alone, and the word is laid out so a narrow chip ellipses the place before it. Under each name,
  where: "with you", "on Chess", "on a toy that isn't in your Lab", "in the Lab", "elsewhere on
  the site", "not showing where they are" — never "not sharing", because a hidden tab and a
  private page look exactly like it, on purpose (party.ts). **Go** opens a friend's toy through
  the picker's own path — the address is replaced, not pushed, so Back is not filled with toys —
  and hands the focus to that toy's own button in the picker, because Go removes itself (on their
  toy, they are "with you"). A Go that vanishes under the focus for any other reason hands it on
  within the strip (`rescueFocus`, as round the toy).

On a phone (under 700px) it is one row — up to four tokens, a line saying where everybody is, and
"Show N" — because twelve 44px rows above a 133-toy picker would push the toy two screens down. The
toggle is a button with `aria-expanded`, not a `<details>` (CLAUDE.md §2's closed-fold trap).

## Step 3 — the tour: Follow

Each chip has **Follow**. Pressing it is a standing, local choice to go wherever that friend goes:
nobody is moved who did not press it, and the leader is never told (shared.ts and together.ts —
sharing never makes anybody follow). `followStep` in `tour.ts` decides each moment, in an order
that is the design:

1. **Stop** when the follow can no longer mean anything: the leader left the call, your gate shut,
   you stopped sharing — or you picked a toy yourself (the picker, Go, a link, the roster's 🧪:
   any address naming a toy). Each says so to a screen reader in words.
2. **Wait**, keeping the follow, while there is nowhere to go: the leader is quiet (a hidden tab
   sends `gone`, so a leader who alt-tabs would otherwise end everybody's tour), on another page,
   on the Lab with no toy said, already with you, on a toy your Lab does not list — or your own tab
   is hidden.
3. **Offer, never move**, when a move would throw something away: a seat in an unfinished
   call-table game (useLabRoom publishes it, `held.ts`), or a match you are viewing (the address
   would be rebuilt without it). The row says "Ann went to Lamps. You're in a game here, so you
   stayed." with a Go that keeps the follow.
4. **Wait for the leader's toy to settle**: `settleMs(people, sends) = max(1000, 400 × people ×
sends)`, where `sends` is what a move onto that toy costs (below). A leader clicking through five
   toys moves followers once.
5. **Keep offering** a toy already offered this move: you are never moved later for it, and its Go
   stays — the seat freeing or the 15 s passing does not turn the offer into silence.
6. **Wait on a crossing**: two people who press Follow on each other at once would swap toys for
   ever. If the leader has just arrived on the toy you just left for theirs, only the one whose id
   sorts first goes, and they meet.
7. **Offer** when you pressed or typed inside the toy in the last 15 s — a press is never answered
   by a jump. A key counts unless the focus is on a control outside the toy, because the toys that
   take keys on the window are played with the focus on the page.
8. **Go**, through GpuLab's `show`, which keeps the follow (`pick`, the picker's path, ends it).

The press itself moves you at once — no settle, no play rule — but never out of a seat or a match,
and it is spent on its first ask: a press made while the leader is away is not a pass for their
arrival minutes later. A toy you were offered stays declined until the leader names another — a
leader who hides their tab and comes back on the same toy has not moved. Follow ends when you leave
the Lab page: a Lab left open in a canvas window over another page is not steered from out of
sight.

### What Follow costs

A move onto a plain toy is one ordinary pick, so one `where`. A move onto one of the 22 toys with
a call table also opens its room: measured on the dev call, one `where`, the room's `hello` and the
host's `state` twice — **four sends** (`sendsFor`; the dev build's StrictMode doubles the hello,
production does not). Realtime meters each send as one message per person on the call. With a
follower moved at most once per `settleMs(N, s)` and a move costing `s` sends, a follower costs at
most `s·N / (0.4·N·s)` = **2.5 messages a second, whatever the call size and whatever the toy**.

⚠️ **The first version of this said 2.5 for every toy while scaling by people alone**, which is
right for a plain toy and four times too low onto a call table: about ten a second per follower,
and past the plan's ~100/s estimate at the cap of twelve with a leader hopping between game toys. A
review counted the room's handshake; the measurement above settled it. The settle onto a call-table
toy is therefore four times as long — 3.2 s for two people, 4.8 s for three — which costs a little
patience at exactly the moment people are about to sit down to a game anyway. `ROOM_TOYS` is the
list, and `tour.test.ts` keeps it true by reading every toy's source for `useLabRoom(` and the
Lab's registry for its id; the cost test counts each move with its toy's own sends, between plain
toys, between call-table toys and between one of each, for every call size from 2 to 12. **These
are estimates against the plan's figures for the Free plan; no meter reading from a real call
exists yet** — one is asked for below.

## Step 4 — dice

**🎲 Surprise us** sits in the strip's header (and beside Call chat in the phone's open list): a
toy chosen at random for the call to go to — any toy your Lab lists except the one on screen (and,
since 27 Nov 2026, Gust: below), from the kind the filter brings forward, or from all of them when
that kind has nothing else (`surprise` in `tour.ts`, seeded in its tests). ⚠️ "Your Lab lists" was an admin's whole picker,
hidden toys included, until `c6ef92fe`: a roll is a pick the whole call is taken to, so the dice now
draw from `rollable` — the toys listed for everybody (catalog.ts's `listed` with admin false) — and
the bus's dice draw from the same list. It is a pick like the picker's, so it ends your own
follow, and anybody following you comes along: the dice are how a leader takes the call somewhere
nobody chose. It adds nothing to the wire beyond the `where` any pick sends.

## The bus — step 0: a game survives somebody with a lower id arriving

The party loop (a guide, a roll, "take me there", stars) brings several friends onto a call-table
toy at once, and planning it found a bug in what is already live. A call-table room's host is
whoever present has the lowest id, and presence makes a newcomer host the moment their `where`
lands — before their own room exists, because the toy's code mounts after. The old host stopped
answering, and the newcomer led from an empty board: their state was older than everybody's and
ignored, while every move went to them and was refused. **A game between two friends stopped the
moment a third, with a lower id, came to watch.**

`answersHello` (room.ts): a newcomer's hello is answered by the host as it was before they arrived,
or by whoever published the room we hold. Measured on the dev call (Gomoku, Bo and Cy three
stones in, Ann's id sorting first): on main Ann's board was empty and Cy's next stone was refused
on every tab; here Ann was handed the three-stone game and Cy's stone landed on all three boards.
In room.test.ts, one lower id, two in either order, and a host who has left the room in somebody
else's hands — each made red by removing its half of the rule — and no extra cost: three sends
for a higher id joining and ten for four filling a fresh toy, exactly as before.

## Step 5 — the bus

A party tour of the Lab. Somebody presses **🚌 Start a party tour** (on a phone, **🚌 Tour** in the
folded row); the guide rolls three toys and picks one; everybody who presses **Take me there** goes,
lowest id first, a moment apart; the turn to roll passes on in boarding order; five stops and a
finale. The rules are `bus.ts` (pure, steps 3–4 of its plan), every sentence is `busWords.ts`, and
this step is the page: `useBus.ts` (listening, the clock, the hop), `BusPanel.tsx` (a block under
the strip's header; on a phone one row outside the fold, like the follow row), `busNow.ts` (the
tour as drawn, for the line above the toy and the chip badges still to come), and `Token.tsx`, moved
out of TourStrip so the scoreboard draws the same token.

**What it keeps to.**

- **Nobody moves who did not press.** A hop is this browser's own press, kept here and never sent,
  for that departure and that toy; a state can only fail to move somebody. Seated in a game still
  going, or watching a match, the yes says what it costs — "🚌 Leave my game and go", "🚌 Leave this
  match and go" — and without that press the hop is an offer instead. An own pick of another toy
  during the window keeps the yes; Stay here, Not this one, Hop off and Follow clear it.
- **Counts, never names, for who is going** ("3 going so far"). The scoreboard names riders,
  because boarding is a pressed, public act.
- **Listening and sending** follow the Lab's gate: read only while your gate allows, you share, and
  you are on the Lab page; sent only while your `where` names a toy (tab visible, a toy on screen).
  When listening stops the panel goes and the strip says why — "Toys are private in this call now…",
  or, when you switched 🫱 off yourself, "You stopped sharing…" (a sentence added to busWords,
  because the first would be untrue then). Follow and the bus share the strip's one status region,
  the latest sentence winning.
- **Nothing periodic.** One `setTimeout` re-armed to the next moment the bus names, and the bus asked
  again whenever who is here, or whether you are live, changes. Never `requestAnimationFrame`.
- **One Lab drives.** A module-level owner token: a second Lab mounted at once draws from `busNow`
  and presses through it.

**Found on the dev call, and fixed, each with a test made red first** (23 Nov 2026):

- **A dev pace shorter than a state can take to arrive.** At pace 0.25 the count was 750 ms, and the
  host sends one state a second, so a departure close behind another state reached every rider
  after its own count — all but the host were told "The bus already left". `knobs()` now keeps the
  windows somebody must answer inside (choose, ready, the boarding minimum, the count) at two state
  gaps or more; at the real pace all four are far above it. The departure said "Leaving … in 0
  seconds" when heard late; it now says it has gone.
- **🏁 Finale offered during the dwell**, where the host refuses `fin` (as it does a roll): pressed,
  nothing happened. The guide now reads "That was the last stop. You open the finale once
  everyone's had a moment at …" until the roll would open.
- **An offer drawn over a turn.** A guide who stayed in a game was offered, and the offer hid "Your
  turn to roll." for the rest of the stop: nobody could roll. An offer now gives way to a line
  with something to do (`lineWith`).
- **A yes the departed host took with it.** Ann said yes, the host closed its tab before its next
  state, and Ann — host now — skipped the window she had said yes to. A yes the window does not
  list is asked again, once of each new host (`askAgain`), within the three a window.
- The hold named its players in the order their seats reached the host; now in turn order.
- On a phone, **🚌 Tour** beside **Show N** crushed the summary line to a word, and the line's text
  then widened the page by up to 360px (a wrapping row's min-content is the whole of a one-line
  text, and the grids above take it as a floor). The two buttons now move to a row of their own
  together, and the line is `contain: inline-size`.

**Why a backstop instead of another case.** Every shape so far — the host's end folded away
(`leftBy`), a lone host back holding nothing (`hostAlone`), a Hop off as the host changes, and these
two — was found one at a time, on the dev call, and fixed as a case. Each fix was right and each
left the next shape open, because they all asked who answers whom, and a page that holds nothing
answers nobody. What every one of them had in common is that the tour's clock ran out and nothing
moved it: whoever decides a tour acts on its own at `due` — the dice choose, the bus leaves or
skips, the tour ends for want of requests — and says so in a state. So, in `bus.ts`'s `stalled`:

> **A tour whose phase is ABANDON_MS past its own deadline, with no state of it heard or sent here
> in that time, is abandoned on every screen that holds it.** That screen offers Start — "The bus
> has stalled: nobody is driving it." when somebody on the bus is here, "Nobody on the bus is here
> right now." when nobody is — and takes the next state of any other tour, whatever it beats, as for
> `leftBy`.

ABANDON_MS past the deadline, not a moment past it, because a host gone silent (no bye) stays on
everybody's Lab until the party's sweep — 16 s stale and a 2 s sweep, 18 s at most — and only then
does the next host take its window over (`claims`). ABANDON_MS is 20 s, so at the real pace the
backstop never fires on a tour somebody is about to take over (tested: the host silent the moment
its window is due and swept 18 s later, and nobody offered Start at any 100 ms). It never fires on
its decider's own screen, which acts at `due`. ⚠️ At the dev call's pace (0.25) ABANDON_MS is 5 s
while the sweep stays 18 s, so there a silent host's window does stall before it is taken over — c3
below shows it.

Three rules go with it, each a shape the backstop alone would catch only slowly:

- **A rider back is not a decider back** (B). A tour given up for want of riders came back the
  moment one was here — but a page back from a reload holds nothing until somebody hands it the tour.
  Once given up, it is back only when a state of it is heard or sent here, or when this browser comes
  to decide it.
- **The lowest here may hold nothing** (A). A page that says hello holds nothing until it hears a
  state, and everybody hears both, so each browser keeps who has asked since the last state
  (`askers`), and `hostAlone` passes them over: the next lowest answers. A hello answers everybody
  still asking, not only its sender, so it does not matter which of two new pages' hellos arrives
  first — on the dev call Dee's came 177 ms before Bo's, and Ann answered 19 ms after his.
- **A tour given up goes back only to its host.** Handed to a newcomer it would be a dead tour on one
  more screen, and every holder hearing the answer would count it as heard of.

**What each costs.** The backstop sends nothing: it reads two clocks this browser already keeps,
and useBus arms one more moment on its one timer. Passing over the askers costs what `hostAlone`
did: one state, from one browser, once a return (asserted). Answering everybody still asking is the
same one state, folded by the Outbox. A tour given up no longer answers newcomers, so it costs less
than before. No new kind and no new field: old clients are unaffected, and the bound on what a
second can hold is unchanged.

of 600 seeds at its first look). And a screen whose tour stalls offers Start rather than showing the
tour its friends moved on to, until that tour next changes — the next state of any other tour, not
